# Creator Economy Problem Research

## Evidence

The official competition frames discoverability, engagement, and workflow efficiency as central creator problems. Patreon reported in 2025 that 78% of surveyed creators said burnout affects their motivation, linking the pressure for constant growth with unsustainable work. YouTube has described recommendation systems as a major driver of viewership and also highlights the need for deeply engaged fan communities. U.S. Copyright Office material on digital assets repeatedly distinguishes token ownership from copyright ownership and identifies unclear rights, infringement, and platform fragmentation as practical problems.

## Problem Selection

Loreline focuses on a specific compound pain:

> A creator with a growing fictional world or community cannot personally remember every canon rule, prior moderation decision, fan contribution, consent boundary, and reuse promise—yet losing that context damages trust and makes valuable co-creation risky.

The existing workflow is fragmented across posts, private notes, chat history, moderation tools, and informal direct messages. Generic moderation tools ask whether a message is allowed. Loreline asks a higher-value question: **Can this contribution safely become part of the creator's world, under what conditions, and with what attribution?**

## Target User

- independent fiction writers and web novelists;
- tabletop and indie game worldbuilders;
- animation, comic, and virtual-world creators;
- community-led intellectual-property projects;
- creator teams running lore contests and fan contribution programmes.

## Why the Pain Is Suitable for Minds

The decision depends on long-lived, creator-specific context. The useful response today may depend on a rule established weeks earlier, a prior exception, a contributor's history, and an unresolved promise. This is exactly where persistent identity, history, follow-up, and tool use matter more than one-off text generation.

## Product Boundary

Loreline is not a legal service and does not decide copyright ownership. It helps creators preserve their own rules, approvals, and attribution records. Any rights receipt is evidence of an explicit workflow decision, not a substitute for a legal agreement.
