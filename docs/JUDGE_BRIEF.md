# Judge Brief

## What is it?

Loreline is a persistent canon and community steward for creator-owned fictional worlds.

## Who needs it?

Writers, game makers, worldbuilders, comic and animation creators, and any creator whose audience contributes lore or derivative ideas.

## What problem does it solve?

Creator communities outgrow the creator's ability to remember every canon rule, exception, fan contribution, and reuse promise. Generic moderation can remove messages but cannot safely turn a contribution into creator-approved canon.

## Why Minds?

The decision depends on old creator context. Loreline uses a stable Mind identity, conversation history, cross-session memory, and later follow-up. Replace the Mind with a one-off response and the central judgment loses its evidence.

## How is memory used?

Session A stores a canon rule. Session B deliberately omits it and receives a revision request. Session C supplies only the revision; the Mind retrieves both the rule and the prior issue before deciding again.

## How is autonomy used?

Open cases carry a due action. An equipped Skill revisits them, evaluates the current state, and prepares the next step without asking the creator to restart the case.

## Why is it different?

It is not a broad creator assistant or generic moderation queue. It manages the boundary where community work may become part of the creator's world.

## Where does Web3 matter?

After approval, a compact receipt lets creators, contributors, and publishers independently verify the contribution and referenced terms. It does not claim ownership or place private content on-chain.

## Does it work?

The production web product, official client integration, three-stage continuity, durable memory, asynchronous reply correlation, and failure handling are working. The receipt contract and wallet states pass local tests. Test-network deployment and platform-initiated autonomy remain `NOT WORKING YET`; no substitute result is presented.

## 60-Second Explanation

A creator states today that the Glass Sea can never be crossed at night. A week later, a fan submits a midnight voyage. Loreline remembers the old rule, explains the conflict, asks for one revision, and keeps the case alive. When the revision is accepted, it preserves attribution. That is valuable only because the Mind remembers the creator's world and continues the relationship across sessions.
